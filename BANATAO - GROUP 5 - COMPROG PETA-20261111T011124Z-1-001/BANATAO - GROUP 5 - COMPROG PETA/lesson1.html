What is Literature?

Literature comes from the Latin word “litera” which literally means an "acquaintance with letters". It is the total of preserved writing belonging to a given language or people.

According to Rexroth (2024), Literature is a body of written works that have traditionally been applied to imaginative works such as poetry and prose with the intentions of their authors and the perceived aesthetic excellence of their execution.

Literary Genres

  • Prose
    • Fiction: Romance, Fantasy, Sci-Fi, Mystery/Thriller, Folklore, Historical
    • Non-Fiction: Biography, Autobiography, Narrative, Periodicals, Anecdote, Essay
  • Poetry
    • Narrative: Epic, Ballad, Metrical Tale
    • Lyric: Song, Ode, Sonnet, Elegy, Haiku
  • Drama
    • Tragedy, Comedy, Melodrama, Farce, Tragicomedy

Poetry

It is a form of literature that uses aesthetic and rhythmic qualities of language.

Literary work in which special intensity is given to the expression of feelings and ideas by the use of distinctive style and rhythm; poems collectively or as a genre of literature. (Oxford Languages)

Elements of Poetry (Form, Sound Devices, Mood)

  • Form: The appearance of a poem. Poems are divided into lines. A stanza is a group of lines.
  • Sound Devices:
    • Alliteration: Repetition of the initial consonant sound.
    • Consonance: Repetition of the intermediate or final consonant sound.
    • Assonance: Repetition of vowel sound.
  • Mood: The feeling that a poem creates in a reader. It can be positive or negative.

Elements of Poetry (Tone, Imagery, Theme)

  • Tone: It is the attitude a writer takes towards the subject or audience of the poem.
  • Imagery: Language that appeals to the 5 senses. It helps the reader to experience familiar things in a fresh way using the senses.
  • Theme: The theme of a poem is its central or main idea.

Types of Poetry

  • Narrative Poetry: A form of poetry that tells a story. It usually follows a plot with characters, a setting, and a climax.
  • Lyric Poetry: A type of poetry that expresses personal emotions or thoughts. It often includes rhyme and meter and can be sung as a song.

Types of Poetry (Narrative)

  • Epic: A long narrative poem that tells about fictional heroic acts.
  • Ballad: Simple narrative poem that tells about emotional happenings.

Types of Poetry (Lyric)

  • Elegy: Lyric poem that deals with life and death.
  • Haiku: A Japanese poem consisting of 17 syllables which is written in 5-7-5 syllables in three lines.
  • Ode: A lyric poem that can be rhymed or unrhymed which is usually addressed to a person, place, or thing with high emotions.
  • Sonnet: A poem which consists of 14 lines and has rhyme and rhythm.

Types of Poetry (Other Forms)

  • Free Verse: A form of poetry composed of either rhymed or unrhymed lines that have no set fixed metrical pattern.
  • Shape Poetry (Concrete Poetry): Develops the physical form of the words on paper. So, a poem about the stars would take the shape of a star (or stars).
  • Acrostic: A poem where certain letters in each line spell out a word or phrase.
  • Blank Verse: Poetry that is written in unrhymed iambic pentameter. Blank verse is often unobtrusive and the iambic pentameter form often resembles the rhythms of ordinary speech.

Figurative Language

Figurative language is defined as word and phrase usage that does not have its normal everyday, literal meaning.

Writers use figurative language to make their work more interesting or dramatic than literal language which simply states facts.

Examples of Figurative Language

  • Simile: Sweet as candy, Cold as Ice.
  • Metaphor: The moon was a ghostly galleon tossed upon cloudy seas.
  • Personification: As we hold tightly, the twinkling stars stare at us.
  • Hyperbole: You could have knocked me over with a feather.
  • Idiom: I brought my wheels with me, It is raining cats and dogs.
  • Onomatopoeia: The snake hissed with anger and fear.
  • Synecdoche: I brought my wheels with me.
