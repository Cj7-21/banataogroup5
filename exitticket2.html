The Last Seed

Every morning before sunrise, Lila walked to the small garden beside their home carrying a small watering can. The dry soil cracked beneath her feet, and the hot wind carried dust through the empty fields. In her hand rested the last seed her grandmother had saved from the previous harvest.

Many families in their village had already stopped planting. The long drought had destroyed their crops, and everyone believed that nothing could grow until the rainy season returned.

One morning, while Lila gently watered the ground, her neighbor laughed.

"Why waste your time?" he asked. "That seed will never grow."

Lila looked at the tiny seed in her hand before placing it carefully into the soil.
"Maybe," she answered quietly, "but I'll never know unless I try."

Every day she returned to the garden. Sometimes she found herself wondering if the others were right. The ground remained dry, and nothing changed. Still, she continued watering the small patch of earth with the little water they could spare.

One evening, dark clouds finally gathered above the village. A gentle rain fell throughout the night.

The next morning, Lila rushed outside. There, pushing through the damp soil, was a tiny green shoot.

Her grandmother smiled.

"Hope grows quietly," she whispered. "Those who are patient are often the first to see it bloom."

Lila smiled back as the morning sunlight touched the young plant. For the first time in many months, the empty field no longer seemed hopeless.
