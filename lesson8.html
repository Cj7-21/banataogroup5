Lesson 8: Literary Analysis

Introduction to Literary Analysis

How do readers move beyond understanding a story to interpreting its deeper meaning?

What is Literary Analysis?

  • Closely studying a text
  • Interpreting its meanings
  • Exploring why the author made certain choices
  • An argumentative essay where you need to analyze elements such as language, perspective, structure of the text, and literary devices

What to do before analysis?

Do a close reading of the text to form a Thesis Statement.

Standard Structure: Introduction → Body → Conclusion

Thesis Statement

  • The point you want to make about the text (Core Argument)
  • Provides the direction of your essay
  • Should be an answer to a question, not just a simple "yes" or "no"
  • Original and arguable; must be complex enough to develop

Steps in Writing: Step 1

Do a close reading of the text and identify the literary devices. Pay attention to the things that are most intriguing, surprising, or even confusing.

Take note of the following:

  • Language Choices: Was the language used by the author simple, complex, or poetic?
  • Narrative Voice & Tone: Who is telling the story and how are they telling it?
  • Structure: Notice how the text is structured and its relevance.

Steps in Writing: Step 2

Formulate a Thesis Statement: The core argument or point you want to make about the text.

Weak Example: The character Frankenstein is portrayed negatively in Mary Shelley’s Frankenstein.

Strong Example: In Frankenstein, Mary Shelley uses the contrasting ambitions and consequences of Victor Frankenstein and his Creature to demonstrate that unchecked scientific hubris and the denial of moral responsibility lead to catastrophic isolation and destruction.

Finding Textual Evidence: Comb through the text to search for relevant quotations that support your thesis statement.

Steps in Writing: Step 3

Writing a Title and an Introduction:

  • The title should clearly indicate what your analysis will focus on (can be written after you start writing).
  • Format: Relevant quote from the text, colon, then the rest of your title, including the name of the author and text.
  • Example Title: Mary Shelley’s Frankenstein: “I Was Benevolent and Good; Misery Made Me a Fiend” — An Analysis of Unchecked Ambition, Abandonment, and the Creation of Monstrosity
  • The introduction provides a quick overview of where your argument is going, including your thesis statement and a summary of the essay’s structure.

Steps in Writing: Step 4

Writing the Body of the Essay:

  • Everything between the introduction and conclusion.
  • Contains your argument and the textual evidence that supports them.
  • Consists of paragraphs structured logically (e.g., Five Paragraphs 1-3-1 format) that remain relevant to the thesis.
  • Use topic sentences, transition words, and proper textual evidence.

Steps in Writing: Step 5

Writing the Conclusion:

  • Do not introduce new quotations or arguments.
  • Summarize key points and emphasize their significance.

Thank You for Listening!
